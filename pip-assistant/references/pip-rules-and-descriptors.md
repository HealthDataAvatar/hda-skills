# PIP Rules, Definitions and Descriptor Tables

**Last verified: 29 August 2026.**

Before using this reference, follow the freshness/source hierarchy in `SKILL.md`. Current legislation, binding/relevant case law and current authoritative guidance override this bundle.

## Precise PIP definitions

Use the current official definition if authoritative guidance has changed. Bundled definitions last verified 29 August 2026:

| Term | Working PIP definition |
|---|---|
| Aid or appliance | A device that improves, provides or replaces impaired physical or mental function; includes a prosthesis. |
| Aided | With an aid/appliance or supervision, prompting or assistance. |
| Assistance | Physical intervention by another person; speech alone is not assistance. |
| Assistance dog | A dog trained to guide or assist a person with sensory impairment. |
| Basic verbal information | Information in the claimant's native language conveyed verbally in a single sentence. |
| Basic written information | Signs, symbols and dates in standard-size written/printed text in the claimant's native language. |
| Bathe | Includes getting into or out of an unadapted bath and shower. |
| Communication support | Support from a person trained or experienced in communicating with people with specific communication needs, including interpreting verbal and non-verbal communication. |
| Complex budgeting decisions | Household/personal budgets, managing and paying bills, and planning future purchases. |
| Complex verbal information | More than one sentence or one complicated sentence in the claimant's native language. |
| Complex written information | More than one sentence of standard-size written/printed text in the claimant's native language. |
| Cook | Heat food at or above waist height. |
| Dress or undress | Includes putting on and taking off socks and shoes. |
| Engage socially | Interact contextually/socially appropriately, understand body language and establish relationships. |
| Manage incontinence | Manage involuntary bowel/bladder evacuation, including collecting devices/self-catheterisation and cleaning afterwards. |
| Manage medication or therapy | Take medication or undertake therapy where failure is likely to cause deterioration. |
| Medication | Medication taken at home prescribed by a registered doctor, nurse or pharmacist. |
| Monitor a health condition | Detect significant changes likely to lead to deterioration and take professionally advised corrective action without which health is likely to deteriorate. |
| Orientation aid | Specialist aid designed to help a disabled person follow a route safely. Ordinary phone satnav is not automatically an orientation aid. |
| Prepare | Make food ready for cooking or eating. |
| Prompting | Reminding, encouraging or explaining by another person. |
| Psychological distress | Distress related to an enduring mental-health condition or intellectual/cognitive impairment. For journey descriptors involving overwhelming psychological distress, apply the stricter current DWP test. |
| Read | Read signs, symbols and words; Braille is not treated as reading for this activity. |
| Simple budgeting decisions | Calculating cost of goods and change after a purchase. |
| Simple meal | A cooked one-course meal for one using fresh ingredients. |
| Social support | Support from someone trained or experienced in helping people engage socially; current guidance may include someone directly experienced in supporting the claimant where the descriptor conditions are met. |
| Stand | Stand upright with at least one biological foot on the ground. |
| Supervision | Continuous presence of another person for the purpose of ensuring safety. |
| Take nutrition | Cut food, convey food/drink to the mouth, chew and swallow; or take nutrition through a therapeutic source. |
| Therapeutic source | Parenteral or enteral tube feeding using a rate-limiting device such as a delivery system/feed pump. |
| Therapy | Qualifying therapy undertaken at home and prescribed/recommended by an appropriate registered health professional, excluding medication and actions that fall within monitoring a health condition. |
| Toilet needs | Getting on/off an unadapted toilet, evacuating bladder/bowel and cleaning afterwards. |
| Unaided | Without aid/appliance, supervision, prompting or assistance. |

## Current descriptor tables

**Use these only after checking that authoritative current rules have not changed. Last verified 29 August 2026 against the current DWP Assessment Guide Part 2.**

## Daily Living Activity 1 — Preparing food

| Descriptor | Points |
|---|---:|
| A. Can prepare and cook a simple meal unaided. | 0 |
| B. Needs to use an aid or appliance to be able to either prepare or cook a simple meal. | 2 |
| C. Cannot cook a simple meal using a conventional cooker but is able to do so using a microwave. | 2 |
| D. Needs prompting to be able to either prepare or cook a simple meal. | 2 |
| E. Needs supervision or assistance to either prepare or cook a simple meal. | 4 |
| F. Cannot prepare and cook food. | 8 |

Scope reminders:
- simple meal for one from fresh ingredients;
- includes opening packaging, peeling/chopping, serving to plate/bowl, hob or microwave cooking/heating;
- carrying items around the kitchen is not part of this activity;
- oven bending is not the test for “cook”; cooking is at/above waist height;
- ready meals by choice do not establish limitation.

## Daily Living Activity 2 — Taking nutrition

| Descriptor | Points |
|---|---:|
| A. Can take nutrition unaided. | 0 |
| B. Needs to use an aid/appliance to take nutrition, or supervision to take nutrition, or assistance to cut up food. | 2 |
| C. Needs a therapeutic source to be able to take nutrition. | 2 |
| D. Needs prompting to be able to take nutrition. | 4 |
| E. Needs assistance to be able to manage a therapeutic source to take nutrition. | 6 |
| F. Cannot convey food and drink to their mouth and needs another person to do so. | 10 |

Scope reminder: this is the physical/functional act of taking nutrition, not general diet quality. Prompting can be relevant where an impairment affects remembering/motivation/active choice to eat.

## Daily Living Activity 3 — Managing therapy or monitoring a health condition

| Descriptor | Points |
|---|---:|
| A. Does not receive medication/therapy or need monitoring, or can manage medication/therapy/monitoring unaided. | 0 |
| B. Needs an aid/appliance to manage medication, or supervision/prompting/assistance to manage medication, or supervision/prompting/assistance to monitor a health condition. | 1 |
| C. Needs supervision, prompting or assistance to manage therapy taking no more than 3.5 hours/week. | 2 |
| D. Needs supervision, prompting or assistance to manage therapy taking more than 3.5 but no more than 7 hours/week. | 4 |
| E. Needs supervision, prompting or assistance to manage therapy taking more than 7 but no more than 14 hours/week. | 6 |
| F. Needs supervision, prompting or assistance to manage therapy taking more than 14 hours/week. | 8 |

Apply the current rules for what counts as medication, therapy, monitoring and qualifying time. Do not count independent therapy time where only assisted time is relevant under current guidance.

## Daily Living Activity 4 — Washing and bathing

| Descriptor | Points |
|---|---:|
| A. Can wash and bathe unaided. | 0 |
| B. Needs to use an aid or appliance to be able to wash or bathe. | 2 |
| C. Needs supervision or prompting to be able to wash or bathe. | 2 |
| D. Needs assistance to be able to wash either their hair or body below the waist. | 2 |
| E. Needs assistance to be able to get in or out of a bath or shower. | 3 |
| F. Needs assistance to be able to wash their body between the shoulders and waist. | 4 |
| G. Cannot wash and bathe at all and needs another person to wash their entire body. | 8 |

Scope reminders:
- bathing includes getting into/out of an unadapted bath and shower;
- shaving/drying are not part of this activity;
- a wet room may evidence inability to access an unadapted shower but apply the current guidance precisely.

## Daily Living Activity 5 — Managing toilet needs or incontinence

| Descriptor | Points |
|---|---:|
| A. Can manage toilet needs or incontinence unaided. | 0 |
| B. Needs an aid or appliance to manage toilet needs or incontinence. | 2 |
| C. Needs supervision or prompting to manage toilet needs. | 2 |
| D. Needs assistance to manage toilet needs. | 4 |
| E. Needs assistance to manage incontinence of either bladder or bowel. | 6 |
| F. Needs assistance to manage incontinence of both bladder and bowel. | 8 |

Scope reminders:
- includes getting on/off an unadapted toilet, evacuation and cleaning afterwards;
- does not include managing clothing, stairs or simply mobilising to the toilet.

| E. Needs assistance to dress or undress their upper body. | 4 |

## Daily Living Activity 7 — Communicating verbally

| Descriptor | Points |
|---|---:|
| A. Can express and understand verbal information unaided. | 0 |
| B. Needs an aid or appliance to be able to speak or hear. | 2 |
| C. Needs communication support to express or understand complex verbal information. | 4 |
| D. Needs communication support to express or understand basic verbal information. | 8 |
| E. Cannot express or understand verbal information at all even with communication support. | 12 |

Scope reminder: this concerns expressive/receptive verbal communication in the person's native language. Memory/retention after communication is not itself the scope of this activity.

## Daily Living Activity 8 — Reading and understanding signs, symbols and words

| Descriptor | Points |
|---|---:|
| A. Can read and understand basic and complex written information unaided or using spectacles/contact lenses. | 0 |
| B. Needs an aid/appliance other than spectacles/contact lenses to read or understand basic or complex written information. | 2 |
| C. Needs prompting to read or understand complex written information. | 2 |
| D. Needs prompting to read or understand basic written information. | 4 |
| E. Cannot read or understand signs, symbols or words at all. | 8 |

Scope reminder: inability must arise from health condition/impairment rather than ordinary illiteracy or unfamiliarity with English. Memory/retention is not itself the scope.

## Daily Living Activity 9 — Engaging with other people face to face

| Descriptor | Points |
|---|---:|
| A. Can engage with other people unaided. | 0 |
| B. Needs prompting to be able to engage with other people. | 2 |
| C. Needs social support to be able to engage with other people. | 4 |
| D. Cannot engage with other people because engagement causes overwhelming psychological distress or behaviour creating a substantial risk of harm to self/another. | 8 |

Scope reminders:
- concerns contextually/socially appropriate face-to-face interaction, body language and establishing relationships;
- assess ability with people generally, not only trusted people;
- preference for avoiding social contact is not enough without impairment-related functional limitation;
- apply the current strict tests for overwhelming psychological distress and substantial risk.

## Daily Living Activity 10 — Making budgeting decisions

| Descriptor | Points |
|---|---:|
| A. Can manage complex budgeting decisions unaided. | 0 |
| B. Needs prompting or assistance to make complex budgeting decisions. | 2 |
| C. Needs prompting or assistance to make simple budgeting decisions. | 4 |
| D. Cannot make any budgeting decisions at all. | 6 |

Scope reminders:
- complex = household/personal budgets, bills and future purchases;
- simple = cost of goods/change;
- this is functional decision-making, not specialist financial knowledge such as mortgage comparison.

## Mobility Activity 11 — Planning and following journeys

| Descriptor | Points |
|---|---:|
| A. Can plan and follow the route of a journey unaided. | 0 |
| B. Needs prompting to undertake any journey to avoid overwhelming psychological distress. | 4 |
| C. Cannot plan the route of a journey. | 8 |
| D. Cannot follow the route of an unfamiliar journey without another person, assistance dog or orientation aid. | 10 |
| E. Cannot undertake any journey because it would cause overwhelming psychological distress. | 10 |
| F. Cannot follow the route of a familiar journey without another person, assistance dog or orientation aid. | 12 |

Scope reminders:
- designed primarily for mental-health, cognitive and sensory limitations;
- separate planning, leaving home/embarking, and following the route;
- physical walking capacity belongs under Activity 12;
- the OPD threshold is high; ordinary anxiety/worry is not automatically OPD;
- ordinary phone satnav/maps are not specialist orientation aids;
- presence of another person by preference is not enough.

## Mobility Activity 12 — Moving around

| Descriptor | Points |
|---|---:|
| A. Can stand and then move more than 200 metres, aided or unaided. | 0 |
| B. Can stand and then move more than 50 metres but no more than 200 metres, aided or unaided. | 4 |
| C. Can stand and then move unaided more than 20 metres but no more than 50 metres. | 8 |
| D. Can stand and then move using an aid/appliance more than 20 metres but no more than 50 metres. | 10 |
| E. Can stand and then move more than 1 metre but no more than 20 metres, aided or unaided. | 12 |
| F. Cannot, aided or unaided, stand or move more than 1 metre. | 12 |

Scope reminders:
- concerns the physical ability to stand and move on a surface normally expected outdoors, including kerbs;
- a wheelchair does not mean the person “stands and moves” that wheelchair distance;
- apply pain, breathlessness, fatigue, gait, falls, speed, recovery and repeatability through the reliability rules.
