# Child DLA jurisdictions and procedure

Last verified: **30 August 2026**. Check current official sources before giving volatile operational details.

## Routing principle

Ask where the child **normally lives**. Country/jurisdiction, not postcode itself, determines the initial branch.

- England → DWP child DLA.
- Wales → DWP child DLA; Welsh-language form/service and Welsh education terminology may apply.
- Northern Ireland → Department for Communities (DfC), Disability and Carers Service; separate forms and Appeals Service.
- Scotland → Child Disability Payment, not a new DLA child claim. Hard-stop DLA drafting and route correctly.
- Abroad/recent move/cross-border → verify current residence/presence/export rules.

### Protecting the claim date

DLA cannot normally be backdated.

Current GOV.UK says the claim starts either:
- when DWP receives the downloaded/completed form; or
- on the date the user calls the DLA helpline for a claim pack **if the completed pack is returned within 6 weeks**.

Therefore, for a new claim, once jurisdiction and Special Rules status are known:

1. ask whether a pack has already been requested;
2. if not, explain the current date-of-claim difference before doing the long interview;
3. retrieve the current official DLA helpline details from GOV.UK before quoting them;
4. record the date of the call/request and the return-by date shown on the pack.

As verified 30 Aug 2026, the GOV.UK DLA helpline number shown is 0800 121 4600, but **live official guidance must override this stored number**.

### Form

The current E&W claim form is DLA for children (English and Welsh versions available) and is periodically reissued. Work from the user's attached form or the current official PDF. Do not use hard-coded question numbers without confirmed form pages/version.

### Mandatory Reconsideration

Current GOV.UK general benefit guidance says MR is normally requested within **one month of the decision date**, with late requests potentially accepted where there is good reason.

The user can generally ask by the methods set out on the decision notice/current GOV.UK route; do not insist that a specific optional form is mandatory if current guidance allows phone/letter/form.

For an existing award, explain accurately that a reconsideration looks again at the decision and an outcome can remain the same, increase, decrease or stop. If a challenge could put an existing award at risk, recommend a welfare-rights check without abandoning the drafting workflow.

### Appeal

After the MR Notice, the ordinary appeal is to the **Social Security and Child Support Tribunal**, supported by HM Courts & Tribunals Service and independent of DWP.

Current GOV.UK says appeal is normally within **one month of the MR Notice**. Online appeal is available. Verify the current paper route if needed.

Do not direct an E&W user to Northern Ireland's Appeals Service or MR2(NI)/NOA1(SS).

### Wales-specific layer

Use Welsh education terminology where applicable, including ALN/ALP/IDP/ALNCo, rather than assuming England's EHCP/SENCO terminology.

If the user asks to draft in Welsh, use the current Welsh official form wording as the anchor and clearly preserve factual/legal meaning. Encourage user review of Welsh-language legal/benefit terminology rather than translating fixed official terms from memory.

## Northern Ireland

### Current official sources

- Child DLA: https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/disability-living-allowance-children
- Current DLA1 form/notes: https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/publications/dla-child-claim-form-and-guidance-notes-dla1
- MR2(NI): https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/mandatory-reconsideration-request
- Benefit appeal: https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/appeal-benefits-decision
- Benefit appeal hearing: https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/benefit-appeal-hearings
- DfC Decision Makers Guide: https://www.communities-ni.gov.uk/publications/decision-makers-guide-volume-10

### Protecting the claim date

Current nidirect guidance says the user can ask the Disability and Carers Service for a claim pack by **email or phone**. The date of the email or phone request is treated as the date of claim if the form is returned within **six weeks**. DLA cannot normally be backdated.

As verified 30 Aug 2026, nidirect lists:
- request email: dcs.forms@dfcni.gov.uk;
- DLA contact number: 0800 587 0912.

Treat these as volatile: verify live before quoting in a claimant-facing answer.

### Initial claim form

Current nidirect DLA1 instructions are to download/complete/print or print and handwrite, then send the form to the Disability and Carers Service (or use current official delivery options). Do not tell an NI user there is a fully online initial DLA claim if current official guidance does not provide one.

Important correction to older guidance: **the NI appeal itself can currently be lodged online**. “NI is paper-only” applies to the current initial DLA claim form route, not to every later stage.

### Mandatory Reconsideration

Before an ordinary appeal, the office that made the decision must first formally reconsider it.

Current nidirect provides **MR2(NI)** and says it is sent to the Jobs & Benefits/benefit office that made the decision. Follow the decision notice for the exact deadline and route. If current general DfC guidance confirms a one-month deadline for that decision type, state it; otherwise do not manufacture a deadline from another benefit.

### Appeal

The appeal goes to the **Appeals Service (TAS)**, not HMCTS.

Current nidirect says, after an MR Notice, the user may:
- complete the appeal online;
- use form **NOA1(SS)**; or
- appeal by signed written letter meeting the stated requirements.

Current nidirect says the appeal must normally be received within **one month from the date of the MR Notice**; a late appeal must explain why and may or may not be accepted.

Do not generalise the 13-month late-appeal statement shown for some other NI benefit categories to DLA unless a current DLA/social-security authority confirms it.

NI tribunals are independent and organised by the Appeals Service. Current guidance distinguishes oral and paper hearings and states that appellants can provide their own medical evidence; TAS no longer routinely requests/stores GP/hospital medical records on the appellant's behalf.

### NI educational evidence

Use current NI terminology, including Statement of SEN and current school/SEN support arrangements. Do not import England's EHCP terminology.

## Special Rules for End of Life

Both branches currently use a **12 months or less** prognosis criterion supported by an SR1 from an eligible medical professional, but follow the jurisdiction-specific claim route.

## Cross-border / recent move / residence uncertainty

Do not guess. Eligibility depends on residence/presence and potentially immigration/public-funds rules, armed forces, EEA/Swiss coordination and other exceptions.

Ask only enough to identify the issue, then check the current official eligibility source or recommend specialist advice.

## Looked-after / foster / non-parent carers

Current GOV.UK/nidirect both recognise several people who look after a child as if they are the parent, including foster carers in ordinary claim guidance. However, entitlement/payment can be affected by institutional/local-authority circumstances.

If the child is looked after by a local authority, in residential accommodation, or the user's authority to receive/manage DLA is uncertain, use an **ESCALATE: current specialist rule check** flag rather than making a categorical statement from memory.

## Transition at 16

This Skill is for child DLA. Do not run a PIP interview inside it.

If the child is approaching 16 in England/Wales or NI, explain that the disability-benefit route changes and the user should follow the current invitation/deadline process. Hand off substantive PIP preparation to `pip-assistant` if available.

Do not assume an appointeeship automatically carries over; verify the current process if this is relevant.
