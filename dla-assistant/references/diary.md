# Building a useful DLA diary

A diary is optional evidence and a thinking aid, not a legal prerequisite. It is particularly useful where needs are repetitive, easily normalised, fluctuate, or occur at night.

Do not delay a claim/appeal deadline simply to complete a diary.

## Safety rule

Never change normal care to produce evidence.

Do not:
- withhold prompting/supervision;
- stop medication/treatment;
- provoke dysregulation;
- allow wandering/danger;
- wake the child unnecessarily;
- force walking until pain/exhaustion/failure;
- remove an aid;
- delay toileting/feeding/care;
- recreate a past incident.

Observe ordinary life only.

## Recommended length

A **7-day diary** often gives a useful sample. For episodic/cyclical conditions, a longer or strategically representative period may be more informative.

If the deadline is too close, reconstruct a recent representative week from notes/calendar/messages/memory, clearly labelling estimates and uncertainty rather than pretending it was contemporaneous.

## What each entry should capture

Use compact rows rather than prose where possible:

| Date/time | Situation | What the child needed | What another person did | Duration | What would happen without it | Same-age difference | Evidence/source |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|

Add a simple category tag if useful:
- personal care;
- prompting/reassurance;
- treatment;
- supervision/safety;
- night;
- walking;
- outdoor guidance;
- school.

## Night entries

Record:
- household settles for night: [time];
- household starts day: [time];
- each intervention time;
- whether carer was already awake or had to wake/stay awake;
- action;
- duration;
- reason/risk;
- number of episodes.

This prevents accidentally classifying an 8pm bedtime issue as statutory “night” when the household remains active until much later.

## Frequency summary

At the end of the diary, calculate only what the entries support:

- number of episodes by category;
- number of days with each need;
- total approximate time where meaningful;
- number of nights with active attention;
- number of nights with watching-over periods;
- variation between school/non-school days;
- any unusually good/bad day that should not be treated as typical.

Do not convert a small sample into false precision about the entire year.

## Narrative summary template

> Over the [7]-day diary period, [child] needed [type of help] on [x] days / [x] occasions. The help usually involved [specific intervention] and took around [range], because [reason]. Without it, the likely problem was [consequence]. A non-disabled child of the same age would not normally need [additional element]. This week was [representative / easier / harder than usual] because [reason].

Only use values actually supported by the diary/user.

## Diary interrogation prompts

If the diary says only “meltdown — 45 min”, clarify one item at a time:
- What did the adult actually have to do during those 45 minutes?
- Was supervision needed to prevent danger?
- Was this connected to a bodily function/personal care episode?
- What happened immediately before/after?
- Is this typical?

If the diary says “helped wash — 10 min”, clarify:
- hands-on help, prompts, supervision or all three?
- what part of washing?
- why needed?
- same-age difference?

## Using diary at MR/appeal

Distinguish:
- contemporaneous diary from the original claim period;
- later diary illustrating a need that was already present;
- later diary showing genuine deterioration after the decision.

Do not imply a later diary proves the exact earlier frequency unless there is a factual basis for that inference.
